# Will I make the connection? Understanding transfers

[עברית](../he/transfers.md) · [MoveUp](../README.md)

A timetable gap is only part of a transfer. You may need to leave a station, cross a road or walk to a different stop before the next bus arrives.

## Start with the walk

Consider this illustrative journey:

| Part of the connection | Example |
| --- | --- |
| First service arrives | 10:00 |
| Next service departs | 10:07 |
| Walk between stops | 5 minutes |
| Time left after walking | 2 minutes |

The seven-minute gap leaves only two minutes after the planned walk. If the first service is three minutes late, the same connection no longer fits those assumptions. This is an example, not a current route or a recommended minimum transfer time.

## Compare the alternatives

Look for a route with fewer changes, less walking or more time before the next departure. The best choice depends on your walking pace, the station layout and how often the next service runs. Add time for crossings, platforms or finding an unfamiliar stop.

## Check the connection while travelling

Read updated arrival information where available, and watch for a change in the next departure. If the connection becomes impractical, compare another route instead of relying on the original plan remaining valid.

## How MoveUp helps

MoveUp includes walking between stops in connection guidance and helps you compare returned route options. It makes a tight transfer easier to recognize. It cannot guarantee that either vehicle arrives on time or that a driver will wait.
